# My Take on AI in Programming

This article will not be original. My opinion is the amalgamation of all the takes I have heard online and in person. I admittedly feel like I rely on AI much more than is healthy. However, if I don't try to use AI as much as possible, I may be left behind as other out compete me. This here exists the dichotomy for current software engineering students. 

## How much should you rely on AI? 

If you look online at AI discussion, chances are you will find two extremist camps very easily: The anti-AI people who wish the invention never happened and using it will cause the down fall of humanity. And the oppsing extreme, the silicon valley tech leaders that promise AI will be the inevitable future and 99% of jobs will disappear in the next year. Like with most debated subjects, the truth lies some where in between. Where in between? No one knows for sure until a decade or so later. So take anything I say here with a grain of salt. Although, I doubt you came here looking to have your opinion changed. 

That being said here is how I think you should look at AI useage. Use AI for productivity, but not if it gets in the way of your understanding. Now this is not a precise rule, nor by me explaining it more will it be much more clear. I like to operate by a rule: if someone were to come up and point at a function, I should be able to explain what that function does in my own words. Now, I don't need to know what every line in the function is doing, having that understanding can be very useful, especially when you go to debug. However, I think that level of understanding is unneccessary. You can get through a lot more by not scrutinizing everything. 

I think that this take leans on the side of being reliant on AI. You are putting some trust in the fact that the function is correct. However, I like to think of it as an API call. With an API or library, you are out sourcing the work to something that someone already made, and trusting that it is correct. The obvious counter to this is that APIs and libraries are far more reliable, and while I admit to this being the case, there are also short comings for the library. First is that you are more reliant on documentation. Documentation which, I am sure that many a developer has experienced, can be challenging to understand and read through. This is why when I am trying to use a new library, I just ask AI how a specific function works, and it provides a well worded and concise explaination that is maybe wrong 1% of the time. Which I think is better than using the often confusing documentation, which will inevitable lead me through a trial and error process trying to learn how the thing actually works.

That leads to another key point. AI is wrong a lot. I think that this is just generally known, AI wants to appear as correct as possible, so it generates the most likely response to your prompt. This is a major short coming. However, it can solved through testing. If you have a solid understanding of how information is manipulated through your program, you should be able to write a test for it. The trial and error process seems to come much less when using AI. There is a danger to this. Small bugs can creep in where you weren't looking. Spending less time with something means you are less intimite with it. Therefore your overlook things and have to suffer later on in development. I think that with modern IDE tools, this is much less of a problem. And sometimes, the AI can actually debug the program faster than you. 

## Will AI Replace Us? 

I'd say give it another decade or so. However, no one really knows. From my personal experience, AI has grown a lot since 2020 with GPT 3. However, in the last two years, I don't think that AI has moved the needle on improving that much, no matter what the bench marks say. This is a tangent, but something I find annoying is that AI companies seem to really dumb down their earlier models when new ones come out. This is a pretty obvious tactic to get subscribers to use the most expensive model so they pay more. But it feels very disengenuous.

## Conclusion

AI fatigue is a funny term. However, I myself have felt it. We find ourselves in the midst of a dramatic change to how white color work is done. And with change always comes growing pains. However, the world has been said to end many time before, and it will be said many times again. Will AI be the end? No. Innovation is generally a positive thing in the long run. Perhaps that lives of some may be derailed by these changes. That is a unfortunate product of our system and I feel quite terrible for those who have to experience it. I myself have felt the pain of joblessness. And I might be back to square one when my current internship comes to an end. No one knows the future. As cliche as it is, we just have to keep trying because that is all we can do.
