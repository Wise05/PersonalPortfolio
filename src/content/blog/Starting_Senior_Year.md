# Starting Senior Year Reflections

As of writing this, I am on day two of the new semester. 
However, despite this, I think that there is actually quite a bit to write about from what I have experienced. 

## Transitioning from My Internship 

Coming out of my internship and back to school has been a strange transition. 
My internship had me trying to grapple with difficult subjects that were far outside of my comfort zone. 
Also, with it being a remote, self-paced internship, I could push forward at the exact pace that I wanted. 
However, with school I am back to the world where the pace I go is primarily determined by the course schedule. 
Needless to say, I feel very ready to jump back into learning and trying hard things, but I cannot because we have only gone over syllabus material. 
So, at the moment I am left frustrated, but also anxious about what is to come and the level of challenge that I am getting myself into. 

Thankfully, several of my classes give you the option on how difficult you want the class to be. 
For example, CS 4540 (AKA Distributed Systems) gives a great deal of freedom on what you choose your final project to be. 
The unfortunate thing for me is that I naturally gravitate towards wanting to do something challenging because it excites me the most. I have experience with this tendency, though, so since it is my fourth year, I should be able to keep myself in check and not bite off more than I can chew. 

## The Anti-Canvas Sentiment 

Many of my professors have seemed to express some sort of effort to keep Canvas out of the loop for assigning or turning in coursework. 
I believe that this is a result of the ShinyHunters hack that happened last spring semester. 
I myself don't really think that Canvas is that great of a platform, as I have experienced a plethora of bugs that required an email to the professor. 
The UI itself is also unintuitive in many cases, especially for organizing your classes. 
There is a reason why the "Better Canvas" extension exists and is very popular. 

## The Vibe Around Vibe Coding 

The overall attitude I see this semester from my professors and students seems to be more and more accepting of AI. 
Many professors give generous limitations to using AI, such as just saying "you will be responsible for your answer." 
The strictest limitation that I have had so far has been a simple "please use AI only on questions we allow" or "tell us the prompt you used." 
This is quite a contrast to my earlier years, when professors asked us to use no AI and said that it was cheating. 
Then slowly they allowed AI use on some problems, but you had to show your prompts. 
If this trend continues, it makes me wonder at what point will higher-level CS education just be utilizing AI, and what will be taught then?

The perspective among students also seems to have shifted. Several people I know who were previously against AI because it would ruin their education are coming around to it. 
Now, those students are utilizing it to help complete their assignments faster and gather information better. 
Of course, this is not universal. People's attitudes still vary wildly. 
I think that the biggest change in people's views has been from the corporate adoption of AI, and the fact that most employers now expect you as a SWE to have experience leveraging it. 
Repeating myself, this of course is not entirely universal. 
However, the majority sentiment points toward AI adoption. 

I personally look forward to seeing how things play out. 
Will the adoption of AI inhibit the long-term skills of the individuals entering the tech industry today? 
Or will those who leverage it the most be pushed further than their peers? 
Will the AI bubble (if it exists) destroy some people's careers? 
At what point will the AI hype die? 
These are questions that I think we will get an answer to in a decade or so. 
And likely by then we will have so many more new questions to answer that we won't even think to look back on how these questions were answered.
